Set Up Your Desk
to Stop the Damage.

Small adjustments to your workspace make a big difference to your neck, back, and energy. Here's what actually matters.

The honest truth: most desk pain isn't from working too hard — it's from holding the same position for too long. Even a perfect ergonomic setup becomes harmful if you never move. These tips work best alongside regular movement breaks.

🖥️ Monitor Height & Distance

Eyes at the top third of the screen

The top of your monitor should sit at or just below eye level — never above it. Looking up even slightly strains the neck muscles that support your head all day.

Arm's length away

Position the screen about 50–70cm from your eyes. If you're squinting to read, increase the text size — don't lean in.

Laptop? Add a stand

Laptop screens are always too low. A riser plus an external keyboard is one of the best investments you can make for your neck.

Tilt the screen slightly back

A slight backward tilt (10–20°) reduces glare and keeps your head in a more neutral position throughout the day.

🪑 Chair & Sitting Position

Hips slightly higher than knees

This tilts your pelvis forward and naturally lengthens your spine — unlike sitting with knees at 90°, which tends to slump the lower back.

Feet flat on the floor

If your feet dangle, use a footrest. Dangling feet put pressure on the underside of your thighs and cut off circulation over time.

Lower back supported

Lumbar support should sit in the small of your back, not mid-back. If your chair lacks it, a small rolled towel works just as well.

Sit close to your desk

Reaching forward is one of the main causes of upper back pain. Sit close enough that your elbows are at desk height without stretching.

⌨️ Keyboard & Mouse

Elbows at 90° or slightly open

Forearms roughly parallel to the floor relaxes the shoulders and prevents tension creeping up the neck. Angled slightly downward is also fine.

Mouse close to the keyboard

Reaching far to the side for a mouse slowly strains your shoulder. Consider a compact keyboard to bring the mouse in closer.

Wrists neutral, not bent up

Your wrists should be flat or slightly downward when typing. Raised wrists compress nerves and are a common cause of tingling in the hands.

💡 Lighting & Glare

Light source to the side, not behind

A window behind you creates glare on your screen. A window behind your monitor means squinting into the light. Position yourself so daylight comes from the side.

Match screen brightness to the room

Your screen shouldn't be brighter than the space around it. Eye fatigue from mismatched brightness contributes to headaches and neck tension.

Use Night Shift after dark

Warm screen tones in the evening help your body wind down naturally. Find it under macOS System Settings → Displays.

📋 Quick Setup Checklist

Run through this once a week

Top of monitor at or just below eye level
Screen 50–70cm from your eyes
Hips slightly higher than knees
Feet flat on floor (or on footrest)
Lower back supported by chair or cushion
Elbows at roughly 90° when typing
Mouse within easy reach — no stretching
Light source to the side, not behind you
Chair close enough that you're not leaning forward
Taking movement breaks at least once an hour
